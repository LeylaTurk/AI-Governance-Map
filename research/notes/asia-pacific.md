# Batch D — Asia-Pacific

Research date: 2026-09-22

## Overview

Fifteen rows: China, Japan, South Korea, India, Singapore, Australia, New
Zealand, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, Malaysia, Taiwan,
Pakistan, Bangladesh. This is the most legislatively active region in the
dataset right now — Vietnam and South Korea both have comprehensive binding
AI laws already in force (2026), Taiwan and Japan both passed national
"basic"/"promotion" laws that are binding as statutes but deliberately
avoid imposing direct enforceable obligations on private companies, and a
wave of Southeast Asian countries (Thailand, Philippines, Malaysia,
Indonesia) have serious draft legislation moving through 2026.

## Things worth flagging for the fact-checker

- **Japan and Taiwan are an unusual category: binding national laws with
  essentially no enforcement teeth.** Both "AI Promotion Act" (Japan) and
  "AI Basic Act" (Taiwan) are real, enacted, in-force statutes — but Japan's
  has no fines (only "name and shame"), and Taiwan's explicitly does not
  bind the private sector, deferring actual enforceable rules to sector
  regulators over a 2-year window. I classified both as "Sectoral or
  partial binding law" rather than "Non-binding guidelines" since they ARE
  statutes, not voluntary guidance — but this is a judgment call the
  fact-checker should scrutinize, since in practical effect (no current
  penalties) they read a lot like voluntary frameworks. Worth deciding
  whether the 5-category schema needs the map's legend to explain this
  nuance, since "binding law" intuitively suggests enforcement exists.

- **South Korea and Vietnam are the two clearest comprehensive, in-force,
  enforceable AI laws in Asia** — both closely EU-AI-Act-inspired,
  risk-tiered, with real fines (Korea's deferred by a 1-year grace period
  to ~2027; Vietnam's grace period runs to March/Sept 2027 depending on
  sector). These are high-confidence rows and probably safe fact-check
  priorities to confirm quickly and move on from.

- **Bangladesh row has a genuine, unresolved contradiction in my sources**
  about whether a National AI Act would be enacted by 2027 or whether
  drafting would only begin by 2028 — I flagged this explicitly in the
  plain-language summary and set confidence to "low." This needs a look at
  the actual adopted policy document (not just draft versions) once
  finalized.

- **China's regulatory model is fundamentally different in structure** from
  most other rows — no single law, but a genuinely binding, actively
  enforced layered stack of specific measures (generative AI, algorithms,
  chatbots, research ethics). I classified it as "Sectoral or partial
  binding law" since that's the most accurate of the 5 categories, but this
  undersells how comprehensive China's *de facto* coverage is across major
  AI use cases — flagging for the fact-checker to consider whether the
  plain-language summary conveys that nuance well enough for a
  non-specialist reader.

- **Australia and New Zealand are both currently "no dedicated AI law,
  rely on existing law + voluntary guidance"** — but both have some
  movement (Australia's PM floated legislating "Australian Standards for
  AI" in July 2026; NZ's opposition Labour party has proposed a platform
  including a new AI regulator). Neither has been enacted, so I kept both
  rows as "Non-binding guidelines or voluntary only," but flagged the
  political movement in the status field since it could change soon.

- **No sources were paywalled or inaccessible in this batch** — all 15
  rows are based on multiple corroborating secondary/law-firm-tracker
  sources (regulations.ai, IAPP, Baker McKenzie, White & Case, etc.); none
  were independently verified against primary official-gazette legal text
  by me directly, which is a general limitation across this whole batch
  given its size, and something the fact-checker should prioritize
  especially for the "comprehensive binding law" rows (Korea, Vietnam)
  where the stakes of getting scope/enforcement details wrong are highest.

## Confidence

South Korea: high. Bangladesh: low (contradiction flagged above). All
others in this batch: medium.
