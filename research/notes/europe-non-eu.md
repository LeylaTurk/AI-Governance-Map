# Batch C — Europe (non-EU / distinct national developments)

Research date: 2026-09-22

## Overview

Seven rows: United Kingdom, Switzerland, Norway, Turkey, Ukraine, Serbia,
plus Italy (added as the one EU member state found to have a materially
distinct binding national AI law, per the task instructions). Norway is an
interesting edge case: technically not in the EU but adopting the EU AI Act
almost verbatim via the EEA Agreement. Most of the rest of non-EU Europe is
in "draft legislation pending" limbo, generally citing the EU AI Act as a
model even when not legally required to follow it (Turkey, Ukraine, Serbia
all explicitly describe EU-alignment as a goal).

## Things worth flagging for the fact-checker

- **Norway's status is contradictory across sources and I flagged it "low"
  confidence.** Some sources describe the Norwegian AI Act (KI-loven) as on
  track to take effect around August 1, 2026 (matching the EU's own
  timeline), while at least one late-July-2026 source explicitly says the
  process "has been delayed." I could not resolve this conflict within this
  research pass — this is a high-priority item for the fact-checker to
  verify against a primary Norwegian government source (regjeringen.no or
  the Storting/Parliament's own tracker), since it directly affects whether
  Norway's row should say "in force" or "delayed, pending."

- **UK classification is a judgment call.** The UK deliberately has no
  AI-specific statute and instead relies on existing sectoral regulators
  applying existing (non-AI) law. I classified this as "Non-binding
  guidelines or voluntary only" since there is no AI-specific enforceable
  regime, but a case could be made this undersells the real teeth that UK
  sectoral regulators (e.g., the ICO under data protection law, the FCA in
  finance) already have when they choose to apply existing law to an AI
  system. Worth the fact-checker's judgment on whether "Sectoral or partial
  binding law" fits better, given real (if not AI-specific) enforcement
  power exists.

- **Switzerland, Serbia rated "No known AI-specific governance" /
  low-medium confidence** because both are genuinely pre-bill — Switzerland
  hasn't even released a bill for public consultation yet (planned by end
  of 2026), and Serbia's most concrete near-term step is a data-protection
  law with AI provisions, not a dedicated AI law. Both are honest "not much
  here yet" answers rather than gaps in my research — flagging this
  explicitly since the task instructions emphasize not stretching to find
  governance that isn't there.

- **Italy row**: per the task instructions I added Italy as the sole
  example of an EU member state with a materially distinct binding
  national AI law (Law No. 132/2025, in force since Oct 10, 2025). I did
  not do deep research on other EU member states (France, Spain, Germany,
  etc.) — if the fact-checker or a later pass wants to check whether any
  other member state has passed something similarly distinct, that would be
  new research, not verification of an existing claim.

- **No paywalled or inaccessible sources in this batch.** All rows are
  based on multiple corroborating secondary/law-firm-tracker sources; none
  were verified against primary official-gazette text directly.

## Confidence

Turkey, Ukraine, UK, Switzerland, Italy: medium. Norway, Serbia: low —
flagged above for priority fact-check attention.
