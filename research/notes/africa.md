# Batch F — Africa

Research date: 2026-09-22

## Overview

Eight rows: South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, Rwanda, Ghana, Morocco,
Tunisia. This batch shows the African Union's Continental AI Strategy
(Batch A) playing out unevenly at the national level: several countries
(Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Egypt, Morocco, Ghana) have serious draft
legislation moving in 2026, while others (Rwanda, Tunisia) remain at the
strategy/policy stage with only general (non-AI-specific) binding law in
place. None of the eight has a comprehensive binding AI law actually in
force as of the research date — this genuinely reflects the "gap" the
overall map is meant to surface, per the task's guidance not to stretch to
find governance that isn't there.

## Things worth flagging for the fact-checker

- **South Africa's draft AI policy was withdrawn in June 2026 after being
  found to contain fabricated/non-existent source references** —
  apparently because AI was used to help draft it without adequate human
  review. This is a genuinely newsworthy, ironic finding (an AI policy
  undone by ungoverned AI use) that the fact-checker and later the
  design-prompt writer may want to highlight, and it should be
  double-checked directly against a primary South African government
  source given how unusual and citable a claim it is.

- **Nigeria's National Digital Economy and E-Governance Bill status is
  genuinely unresolved** — logged in `research/needs-followup.md`. Every
  source describes it as passed by the National Assembly and awaiting
  presidential assent (originally expected around Nov 2025 - March 2026),
  but nothing I found confirms whether President Tinubu has actually signed
  it as of Sept 2026. This is the single highest-priority item in this
  batch for the fact-checker to resolve, since it would change Nigeria's
  category from "Draft legislation pending" to "Comprehensive binding law"
  or "Sectoral or partial binding law" if it has in fact been signed.

- **Egypt's draft AI Law timeline is similarly uncertain** — sources said
  it was "expected to be finalized and adopted by parliament in
  2025-2026," which I could not pin down further. Not logged as a hard
  access failure (multiple sources agree on the general "in progress,
  expected soon" status, just not an exact date), but flagged here as a
  status worth re-checking.

- **Rwanda and Tunisia are honest "not much binding AI-specific governance
  yet" answers** — both have real national AI strategies and advisory
  bodies but no dedicated AI law, and both rely on general data-protection
  law for the only real enforceable protection touching AI. This is
  consistent with the task's expectation that this will genuinely be true
  for many countries and shouldn't be stretched.

- **All eight countries' rows are based on secondary sources (law firm
  trackers, IAPP, Bloomberg, regulations.ai, government strategy PDFs)
  cross-checked across 2-3 sources each; none were verified against
  primary official-gazette legal text directly by me.**
