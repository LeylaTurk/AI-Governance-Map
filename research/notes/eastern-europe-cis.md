# Batch G — Eastern Europe / CIS

Research date: 2026-09-22

## Overview

Two rows: Russia, Kazakhstan. Both passed their first dedicated AI-specific
federal laws within the last year, but with very different character —
Kazakhstan's is a genuinely broad, risk-based, consumer/rights-protective
framework (bans on manipulative AI and social scoring, mandated content
labeling, a dedicated ministry), while Russia's is narrower and more about
AI sovereignty and content/ideology control (a special "sovereign AI model"
category that must be built domestically and legally required to reflect
"traditional Russian values") than about protecting people from AI harms in
the way the EU/Kazakhstan/South Korea models do.

## Things worth flagging for the fact-checker

- **Russia's law is categorically different in purpose from most other
  "binding AI law" rows in this dataset**, and the plain-language summary
  tries to make that clear rather than implying it's a rights-protective
  regime like the EU AI Act. This is a judgment call worth the
  fact-checker's and design-prompt-writer's attention: a simple 5-tier
  color-coded map risks conflating "binding law that protects AI users"
  with "binding law that asserts state control over AI ideology/sovereignty"
  under the same "Sectoral or partial binding law" category. Worth
  flagging for whoever writes the final map's legend/tooltips.

- **I did not find explicit fine/penalty amounts for Russia's law** in the
  sources available — flagged as a gap in the enforcement_mechanism field
  rather than guessed. Should be checked against the primary Federal Law
  text if a translated version is available.

- **Kazakhstan's row is comparatively higher-confidence** — multiple
  consistent sources (law firm trackers, IAPP-adjacent, AIPPI) describe the
  same substantive picture (risk-based, bans, labeling, dedicated ministry,
  in force since Jan 2026).

- **No paywalled or inaccessible sources in this batch.**

## Confidence

Both rows: medium.
