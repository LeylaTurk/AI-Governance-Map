# Batch A — International & regional bodies

Research date: 2026-09-22

## Overview

Eight rows: EU, OECD, Council of Europe, UN, G7 (Hiroshima AI Process),
ASEAN, African Union, and a combined GPAI/Bletchley Declaration/AI Safety
Summit row. Of these, only the EU AI Act is a binding, in-force,
comprehensive law today. Everything else in this batch is either voluntary
guidance/principles, a strategy document, or a binding treaty that has been
signed but is **not yet in force**.

## Things worth flagging for the fact-checker

- **EU AI Act timeline is a moving target.** Search results mention an "AI
  Digital Omnibus" package that reached provisional political agreement in
  May 2026 and reportedly pushed back some high-risk-system deadlines. I
  could not find a primary EU source (Official Journal / European
  Commission press release) confirming the exact revised dates — this needs
  a fresh, primary-source check. The dates I logged (2 Feb 2025 prohibited
  practices, 2 Aug 2025 GPAI model rules, 2 Aug 2026 general application, 2
  Dec 2027 high-risk rules) are the *pre-Omnibus* baseline from Article 113
  and should be re-verified against the actual Omnibus text.

- **Council of Europe Framework Convention is easy to mis-state.** It reads
  as "the world's first binding AI treaty," which is true in intent, but as
  of the search date (7 Sept 2026) it is **not in force** — only the EU has
  ratified it (1 of the 5 needed, only 2 of 3 needed CoE-member ratifications
  so far since the EU itself isn't a CoE "member state" ratification in the
  same sense — worth the fact-checker double-checking the exact ratification
  math, since the treaty text's entry-into-force clause is a bit fiddly
  about EU vs. member-state ratifications). I categorized it as "Draft
  legislation pending" since none of the five governance-category labels
  cleanly fits "signed treaty, not yet in force" — flag this classification
  choice for review; "Non-binding guidelines" would understate it, since
  it's designed to bind once ratified.

- **GPAI / Bletchley / Paris / Seoul is genuinely a tangle of overlapping
  initiatives** — GPAI (Global Partnership on AI, founded 2020) formally
  merged into the OECD in 2024; the Bletchley Declaration (Nov 2023) kicked
  off a separate but related summit series (Seoul 2024, Paris 2025,
  Bratislava/GPAI Summit Nov 2025). I folded these into one row since they
  overlap heavily in membership and voluntary, non-binding character, but a
  case could be made to split "GPAI" (now effectively an OECD program) from
  "the Bletchley/AI Safety Summit process" (a separate diplomatic track) —
  worth the fact-checker's judgment call on whether to split this row.

- **UN AI governance is very new and still being stood up.** The
  Independent International Scientific Panel on AI and Global Dialogue on
  AI Governance were only created by UNGA resolution in August 2025 — as of
  research date they may not have produced any substantive output yet. This
  row will likely need updating again soon as these bodies start operating.

- **EU member states with distinct national AI laws beyond the AI Act:**
  did not find evidence of any EU member state enacting a *materially
  distinct binding national AI law* that goes beyond/around the EU AI Act —
  EU member states are bound by the AI Act as a directly-applicable
  Regulation (not a directive), which structurally limits how different
  national AI-specific laws can be. Some member states (e.g., Spain with
  AESIA, its AI supervisory agency; Italy with a 2025 national AI law
  focused on implementation/sandboxes) have created *implementation and
  oversight bodies or complementary framework laws* rather than competing
  substantive rules — worth a quick fact-check pass on Italy specifically
  (search results suggest Italy passed a national AI law in 2025 that
  works alongside the EU AI Act, but I did not verify this directly in this
  batch and did not add it as its own row — flagging for possible follow-up
  in Batch C when covering non-EU Europe, or the fact-checker may want to
  add an Italy row).

## Confidence

All eight rows are "medium" — I used a mix of primary sources (coe.int,
oecd.org, au.int, asean.org, digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu, un.org,
soumu.go.jp) and reputable secondary trackers/summaries, and cross-checked
key facts against 2+ sources per row, but did not exhaustively verify every
date or every actor's exact prerogatives against primary legal text. No
sources were paywalled or inaccessible in this batch.
